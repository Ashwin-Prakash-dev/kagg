# Object-size bin definition

Generated by `scripts/analyze_object_sizes.py`. This fixes the `small` / `medium` / `large` grouping used for every `AP_small` / `AP_medium` / `AP_large` number in the study.

## Decision

**Primary: COCO absolute pixel-area thresholds, measured on the ORIGINAL-resolution annotation.**

```text
small   area <  32^2  =  1,024 px^2
medium  1,024 <= area < 96^2 = 9,216 px^2
large   area >= 9,216 px^2
```

## Why these thresholds

1. **Comparability.** They are the thresholds behind every published `AP_S`/`AP_M`/`AP_L` number in the detection literature. Inventing project-specific cut points would make this study's small-object result impossible to place against prior work, which is the main thing a resolution study is read for.

2. **They are not degenerate on this data.** Applied to the 58,982 clean FAIR1M ship boxes they split the population 42.4% / 47.1% / 10.5%:

| Bin | Threshold (px²) | Ships | Share |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| small | < 1,024 | 24,998 | 42.4% |
| medium | 1,024 – 9,216 | 27,774 | 47.1% |
| large | ≥ 9,216 | 6,210 | 10.5% |

   Every bin holds thousands of instances, so per-bin AP is estimable rather than noise. A threshold set that dumped 95% of ships into one bin would have had to be rejected in favour of quantiles; that is not the case here.

3. **They line up with the sensor.** At the FAIR1M median GSD of ~0.81 m, the 32 px boundary is about 26 m on the ground and the 96 px boundary about 78 m. Those are close to the real transitions between small craft (motorboats, fishing boats), mid-size working vessels, and large cargo ships — so the bins carry physical meaning, not just pixel bookkeeping.

4. **They match the detector's own scale structure.** YOLOv8 predicts on P3/P4/P5 feature maps at strides 8/16/32. A 32 px object spans ~4 cells at P3 and a 96 px object ~3 cells at P5, so the bins roughly correspond to which head is responsible for the object.

## Secondary: dataset tertiles (sensitivity check)

Equal-count tertiles of the same population fall at **682 px²** and **2,760 px²** (≈26×26 px and ≈53×53 px). Both binnings are recorded per object in the ship dataset so results can be reported either way. The tertile split guarantees equal support per bin, which the COCO split does not; if the two disagree, that disagreement is itself a finding and must be reported rather than resolved by picking the nicer number.

## The bins are fixed at original resolution and never recomputed

This is the load-bearing methodological choice of the whole resolution × object-size analysis.

Each ship is assigned its bin **once**, from its original-resolution area, and keeps that label in every experiment E00–E03. Bins are attached to the object identity `(Img_ID, Label_ID)`, not recomputed from the resized boxes.

The alternative — re-deriving bins at each resolution — would be wrong. Downsampling scales area by `s²`, so at r25 an object's area falls to 1/16 of its original value and almost the entire population slides into `small`. Each row of the resolution × size table would then describe a different set of ships, and a change in `AP_small` down the column could not be separated from reclassification. Fixing the bins keeps every row about **the same ships**, so a drop is attributable to lost image detail.

## What happens to each bin as resolution falls

Area scales with the *square* of the linear factor: r50 keeps half the linear size but only **25%** of the pixel footprint; r25 keeps **6.25%**.

| Resolution | Bin | Ships | Median box (px) | Median area (px²) | % with longest side < 8 px |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| original | small | 24,998 | 22 × 19 | 424 | 0.0% |
| original | medium | 27,774 | 55 × 50 | 2,680 | 0.0% |
| original | large | 6,210 | 146 × 123 | 16,114 | 0.0% |
| r75 | small | 24,998 | 16 × 14 | 238 | 0.6% |
| r75 | medium | 27,774 | 41 × 38 | 1,508 | 0.0% |
| r75 | large | 6,210 | 110 × 92 | 9,064 | 0.0% |
| r50 | small | 24,998 | 11 × 10 | 106 | 6.5% |
| r50 | medium | 27,774 | 28 × 25 | 670 | 0.0% |
| r50 | large | 6,210 | 73 × 62 | 4,028 | 0.0% |
| r25 | small | 24,998 | 6 × 5 | 26 | 68.5% |
| r25 | medium | 27,774 | 14 × 12 | 168 | 0.0% |
| r25 | large | 6,210 | 36 × 31 | 1,007 | 0.0% |

## Reporting requirement

Any table or plot of `AP_small` / `AP_medium` / `AP_large` in this study must state the thresholds inline (`small = area < 1024 px² at original resolution`), because the numbers are meaningless without them.
